Level 05 · Slot game · Stake Engine

DOPAMINE.exe

Kinga · solo · 2026
Rec
Storybook run · DOPAMINE.exeLoop · 16 s

An original 5×4, 20-line slot built for Stake Engine, the platform where independent creators publish casino games for Stake. The concept: corrupted software, a dopamine loop, degen internet culture. The interface degrades as you win — a DOPAMINE METER climbs from 0 to 100 and the machine goes from calm green to red alarms, glitches and chaos.

The scatter is a red button labelled DO NOT PRESS. The wild is WILD.exe. The bonus is DOPAMINE OVERLOAD: free spins with multiplier wilds, ×2 to ×50. A lab goblin watches from a tank next to the reels and goes through five moods from bored to manic. The spin button evolves from STIMULATE to MORE to DO IT.

Two SDKs, learned from zero. The math lives in the Python Math SDK: symbol strips, paytable, bonus logic, an RTP optimiser (in Rust) tuned to exactly 0.965, a 5000× win cap, and the books of pre-simulated outcomes that the engine actually serves.

The frontend is the point. The Web SDK (Svelte 5 + PIXI 8) ships a generic mining theme; almost all of it was replaced with hand-built HTML overlays: the machine frame with its alarm sequence, the control bar, the bonus panels, the goblin rig with a typewriter speech bubble, and eleven symbols drawn as CSS art and rasterised with headless Chrome. The result runs end to end in the storybook, including a $5,000 win-cap book.

Level 06 · Immersive party game · Halloween

Opuszczona Nałęczowska

Game night · 30 Oct 2026
Phone · projectors · TV
Chapter I · The ball beginsEveryone gets their circle

An original story game for twenty guests, one evening, one real apartment — part theatre, part escape room, part detective story, part 1920s casino. Everyone is the ghost of a guest from a masked ball on 30 October 1926 that ended with the house on fire. A hundred years later the Croupier is back for the souls on that night’s IOU, and the only way out is to name who set the fire. The arsonist is among the players — and doesn’t know it.

Layer 01

Phone

Each guest’s private command centre. Nothing to install — a QR code from the invitation on the TV.

Layer 02

Two projectors

Turn the living-room walls into the cellar, ballroom, bedroom and attic, with film scenes between chapters.

Layer 03

TV

The voice of the house: game board, case files and the IOU clock counting down two hours. Every screen live, in the same second.

Mechanics

What guests do

Circles

Four teams — hearts, diamonds, clubs, spades — revealed only on your phone. Find yours in the room.

Race to the safe

Ciphers, a portrait-wall riddle and a ghost’s question only rival informants can answer.

Missions

Personal tasks closed with codes hidden in the flat or another player’s seal.

Investigation

Private clues to keep, gift, sell or trade, and a prosecution file on your phone.

Croupier’s casino

One-on-one duels for gold or a shot, on a custom art-déco deck.

Phone from beyond

The cellar switchboard calls random guests. Whoever picks up hears something meant only for them.

Penalties

The three poorest souls

After every floor: a ten-second old-cinema countdown, then karaoke with lyrics on the TV, a charleston, or live dubbing of a silent film.

Finale

Blackjack with the Croupier

A shared table projected on the wall, every guest at a seat picked on their phone. Name the arsonist and the clock stuck at 23:47 starts again. Fail, and the ball goes on forever.

Built from scratch. Script, mechanics and art direction are original — sepia portraits of every guest, generated scenes with each player’s face, gilded cards, a recorded narrator. It runs on one laptop in the home network, and the host drives the evening from a control panel.
