Pirate Cove

Six weeks, four people, zero Unity experience on day one. Pirate Cove is the 2D platformer where we learned the engine, game design and level design the only way that sticks — by shipping a game with a lobby, three levels, a secret one and a save system.

The pitch

A pirate washes up in a cove full of treasure, traps and very rude crabs. You start in a lobby hub with three save slots, buy access to the next level and a potion or two, and go.

Each level is a hand-built tilemap with its own hazards: vanishing walls, water you really should not fall into, death zones, super chests. Checkpoints are pirate flags. Enemies walk, shoot or — in the case of Frab the NPC — charge you a tax every time you pass.

The whole point of the project was to learn Unity by finishing something. Six weeks later we had a playable build, 600 commits and the team that went on to make everything else on this site.

Systems

What we built

System 01

Lobby & save slots

A hub with three save slots, save/load, and a shop where coins buy the next level and potions. The first time any of us wrote persistence.

System 02

Levels & hazards

Tutorial, three main levels and a hidden secret level, all on tilemaps. Vanishing walls, water zones, death zones, super chests, keys, skulls and nuggets to find.

System 03

Game feel

Dust on run, jump and landing, water splashes, pickup effects, clouds drifting on every level, a checkpoint flag that actually rises. Small things that made it feel like a game.

Art

Base assets are PixelFrog's Treasure Hunters pack. Everything it did not have, we drew ourselves: the death-in-water animation, the sword death, the rising checkpoint flag, closed doors for each level, amethysts, vines and the whole Grab character with idle and fall frames.

What we learned

  1. Level design is a technical job: pacing, sightlines and flow live in the tilemap, not in a document.
  2. Prefabs per level, per door and per chest saved us from repeating the same mistake in four places.
  3. Git with four beginners works if every feature has its own branch and someone merges every day.
