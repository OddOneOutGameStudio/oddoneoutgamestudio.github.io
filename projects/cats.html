Idle Cat Farm

A small, unhurried farming sim where the cats do the work. They plant, water, refill the well and take naps when they run out of energy, while you pick the crops, fill orders and turn a wild little island into a farm. Written in plain Java and Swing, with no engine at all.

The pitch

You don't farm, you run the farm. Pick a crop at a sign and the cats take it from there: they walk to the field, till, plant and water it, carry the watering can back to the well when it's empty and keep the animals' water trays full. Work costs energy, so a tired cat drops what it reserved and goes for a nap before it works again.

Your part is the planning. Collect eggs from the coop and milk from the barn, shake the orchard trees yourself (too much fun to delegate), fill customer orders and spend the coins on upgrades. Six areas (park, coop, barn, fields, house and orchard) grow through five stages each, from overgrown wilderness to a farm running at full tilt.

Systems

What I built

67 classes · 14k lines of Java
AI

A cat with a to-do list

Every cat runs its own state machine (idle, planting, watering, going to the well, refilling, filling a tray, tired, going to sleep, sleeping) and spends energy on every step of work.

Pathfinding

A* on the tile grid

Cats find their own way to fields, the well and their beds with a hand-written A* over the farm's tile map.

Engine-less

Own game loop

A fixed 60 updates per second on its own thread, rendering only after an update and sleeping off the rest of the frame. Camera, input and drawing order are all by hand in Swing.

World

Text-file tilemaps

The island is assembled from text-based tilemaps, layer by layer. Each of the 24 upgrades rebuilds the world from its maps, so the farm visibly grows.

UI

No menus

The interface lives on the farm itself: watering cans over dry fields, a star over an area ready to upgrade, a coin over the workstation.

Content

20+ crops, orders & animals

Crops unlocked as the farm grows, an orchard with a chance-based shake, eggs and milk varieties, and randomised customer orders to fill.
