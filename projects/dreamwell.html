The pitch

Earth after the nuclear war. You are the last resident of a bunker under the ruins of a small town, keeping the oxygen and power generators alive. Then you find the Immersion Pod — and discover that whatever you gather inside a game, you can bring back out.

The loop is simple to say and hard to balance: enter the bunker → survive → enter a game → explore → return. Every trip costs power and food in the real world, so you are managing two economies, two inventories and one tired survivor.

DreamWell is our engineering thesis at PJATK (XR Games specialisation). We presented it at PJATK Showcase in June 2026 and at the Women in Tech science fair, and we are finishing it this semester.

Three worlds · three looks

Where you play

World 01

The Bunker

Your hub, built from 3D models rendered down to PNG sprites. Power and oxygen generators, a workstation, storage, the 3D converter and a computer with its own apps for managing the bunker and your collection.

World 02

ShimmerWoods

A pastel pixel-art game-inside-the-game. Gather, farm, fish, cook, craft and follow Astrea’s quests. Hedgehogs and sheep run on behaviour graphs.

World 03

BlackNode

Cyber-glitch dungeon with a procedurally sequenced chain of rooms: safe, enemy, resource, treasure, boss. Combo melee, ranged, dash, difficulty that climbs with you — and Elion, the only one who talks down here.

Systems

What we built

~600 commits · 4 people
Inventory

Hotbar, backpack, storage

Drag & drop, stacking, splitting, auto-stack across containers, click-to-pick-up and place, drop-to-world. Two separate inventories — the bunker’s and the in-game one for ShimmerWoods and BlackNode — with their own item sets: pixel-art items inside the games, clean renders in the bunker. Each world has its own HUD.

Crafting

Tiers & workstations

Recipes with tier gating, a crafting table in the woods and a workstation in the bunker, cooking at the campfire, upgradable bunker structures.

Survival

Stats that never stop

Hunger, health, oxygen and power tick everywhere; each world adds its own pool (Spirit, Hearts). Death costs part of your inventory and sends you home.

Architecture

Services & events

Plain C# services behind a service locator, a typed event bus, scene catalog and a game-flow pipeline. Refactored mid-project without losing the game.

Persistence

Save system

Autosave, migrations, per-world state snapshots, round-trip tests. Quests, dialogues, collection and crafting progress all survive a reload.

Art

Pixel art & 3D

Pixel art for the two game worlds, 3D models rendered to sprites for the bunker, UI designed in Figma, shaders and outlines — every asset drawn or modelled by the team.

What we learned

  1. Singletons are fast on day one and expensive on day two hundred. Moving to services and a typed event bus halfway through was painful and worth it.
  2. Three looks in one game only work if the rules for UI are written down. We ended up with an actual UI rulebook.
  3. Four people on one Unity project survive on small branches, one PR per fix and a smoke test before every merge.
